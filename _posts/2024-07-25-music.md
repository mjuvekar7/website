Music has been a big part of my life for as long as I can remember. Much of that
has been in the form of playing the piano, which as of right now I have done for
a little over 14 years. This post has a general list of things that reflect the
state of my musical life right now, including what I am working on, some
recordings from the past ~6 years, and what I would like to play at some point
in the future.

## What I currently enjoy playing

##### In progress

These are pieces I am actively working on right now.
* Debussy, _[Reflets dans l'eau](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dE413i1eAso&t=256s)_
  from _Images_. This is the first significant Debussy work I have played, and
  I really enjoy it. I might work on the rest of Debussy's _Images_ later this
  year.
* TBD - currently thinking about what to work on next; candidates include
  something by Rachmaninoff (because I have never played Rach before), or maybe
  Beethoven's _Waldstein_ sonata.

##### Recording/Mixing/Arranging

* John Hasselback III et al., St. Thomas. \[[audio](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ebpt7eU1dJ6wvJHWX6v-apdha1-oTGWD/view?usp=sharing)\] \\
    The final group project for a recording tech class I took, and the first

    much goes into producing music.
* POch3lbel's Cannon in D (For The Most Part). \[[audio](/assets/audio/PO33.mp3)\] \\
    by mjuvekar and shardulc (2000-), inspired by Johann Pachelbel (1653-1706).
    \[We clearly had too much free time during the pandemic.\]

    _A note from the arrangers_

    We set out with one goal&mdash;to combine the elegance and intricacy of
    Baroque music with the flexibility and experimentalism afforded by
    modern electronic instruments. Upon much research, we found that among
    the vast, rich Baroque repertoire, Pachelbel's
    [Canon in D](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JvNQLJ1_HQ0) offered the most
    fertile grounds for further development, due to its recognizability,
    versatility, centuries of history, and solid foundations in the cello line.
    Drawing inspiration from classics such as [Pachelbel's
    Chicken](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=khOfSVULtsU) and
    [Hlavní nadraží Praha - znělka 02 (Dlouhá
    verze)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cD3QlR98--A), we embarked on the
    bold quest of arranging this iconic piece for the humble [Pocket Operator
    (PO-33)](https://teenage.engineering/products/po).

    The first step was to obtain the perfect audio samples: we tried a
    creaking door, we tried a garbage disposal, we tried a running faucet,
    but finally what really clicked was the sound of a groan from shardulc's
    mucus-lined throat. Then we took a look or two at the score
    ([an authentic source](/assets/pdf/pachelbel_manuscript.pdf))
    and let the creative juices flow. One thing led to another, and in time,
    the fruit of our labor was ripe. We are proud to present a work that we
    believe is truly representative of today's age. We hope you enjoy (?) it.

    &mdash; shardulc &amp; mjuvekar, 2020-08-01
